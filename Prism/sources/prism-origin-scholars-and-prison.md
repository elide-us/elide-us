## v1 — the original telling (colors discovered; "the world shattered" under the strain)

In the ancient times of Prism, before the world was split into its seven shards, there existed only two forces: Life and Death. These primal energies were the domain of the gods, the sovereign wielders of White and Black magic. White magic, the magic of life, flowed from the gods as a force of creation and preservation, while Black magic, the magic of death, was its inevitable counterpart, a force of decay and transformation. Together, these magics sustained the balance of existence, the eternal cycle of birth, growth, and death.

For ages, the gods ruled over all creation with these magics, believing them to be the only forces necessary for the cosmos. Life and Death shaped everything, and the world thrived under the simple duality. However, within this perfect order, a group of scholars, mortals and lesser divine beings alike, began to see the potential for something more. They believed that the magic of White and Black, while powerful, was not the only source of truth in the universe. There were nuances in the light and shadow, in the spaces between life and death, that the gods had overlooked.

These scholars, seen as rebellious heretics by the gods, began to experiment in secret. They discovered that by extracting elements from both light and shadow—neither fully aligned with life nor death—they could manipulate the fabric of reality in entirely new ways. From their defiance came the first discovery: fire. Drawing from the destructive force within Black magic and the warmth of creation within White, they birthed Red magic, or Pyromancy, which burned with both the potential to create and destroy.

Emboldened by their success, the scholars continued their work, peeling away layers of existence from the boundaries of life and death. They found that chaos itself, the unpredictable forces of the cosmos, could be harnessed. Thus, Orange magic, the magic of Chaos, was born—a power that could warp reality and unleash the wild, untamed forces of the universe. From this magic, storms were conjured, and the world became a more dynamic, living place.

With the discovery of Red and Orange magic, the rebellious scholars pressed on, and soon, they uncovered other colors of the spectrum. From the swift energy of lightning and the air itself, they wove Yellow magic, a force of speed and power, a connection between the heavens and the earth. Green magic followed, as they tapped into the pulse of nature itself, finding the essence of growth, renewal, and the living world. Lastly, they pulled from the deep waters and the eternal flow of time to create Blue magic, mastering the currents of water and the fluidity of change.

As the scholars perfected their work, the balance between life and death began to shift. No longer did the world exist solely in the binary of creation and destruction. These new magics introduced the complexity of nature, the unpredictability of chaos, the fury of fire, and the fluidity of water. It was said that the very fabric of reality strained under the weight of these new forces, until finally, in a cataclysmic event, the world of Prism shattered. Seven shards were cast across the cosmos, each one imbued with the essence of a particular magic.

The gods, furious at the scholars' defiance, retreated to the shards, each taking up the mantle of guardian over one of the newly formed schools of magic. Now, the gods are no longer the sole wielders of power. They guard their shards jealously, overseeing the magic they once feared and loathed but now must protect. The once-perfect world of White and Black had fractured, and with it, a new era began—a world of color, complexity, and endless possibility. Thus, the rebellious scholars' legacy endured, forever shaping the destiny of Prism and the magic within it.

## v2 — the prison retelling (gods as administrators; still says the scholars' magic fractured the world)

In the myth of Prism, the world was not merely a vibrant plane of magic, but a prison—a celestial stronghold designed to contain and control. The gods, who once ruled with the powers of Life and Death, had not created Prism as an act of divine craftsmanship, but as a prison for those who sought to defy their order. These gods, now revealed as prison administrators, were tasked with maintaining the cosmic balance and ensuring that no force would rise to challenge their dominion over existence.

The rebellious scholars who unlocked the magic of Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, and Blue were not merely seekers of knowledge—they were prisoners, confined to Prism for their ambition and defiance. Their discovery of the magics beyond White and Black was not just a breakthrough but an act of rebellion against the constraints the gods had placed on them. Each new color of magic represented a tear in the fabric of their imprisonment, a step closer to freedom from the absolute authority of Life and Death.

But the gods were not content to let their prisoners shatter their chains. When the scholars' magics caused the world to fracture into seven shards, the gods seized the opportunity. Each shard, now imbued with a distinct form of magic, was placed under the strict watch of one of these divine administrators, who ruled over their territories with an iron hand, ensuring that the prison would remain sealed.

Each god, now a warden, guarded their respective shard of Prism, preventing any one force from uniting the magics and breaking the prison entirely. The gods' roles were not just to oversee, but to control, to prevent the flow of power between the realms, ensuring that none could ever wield the full spectrum of magic again. To this day, the seven shards of Prism remain divided, and the gods stand vigilant over their charges, ever watchful for signs of rebellion.

The magic of Prism, while once a symbol of hope for freedom, now serves as a reminder of their imprisonment, guarded fiercely by the very beings who once wielded the simpler forces of creation and destruction. Thus, the world of Prism stands as both a prison of light and shadow, with its inhabitants caught between the ancient defiance of the scholars and the unyielding authority of the gods.

## v3 — the corrected telling (the gods shattered the world to pre-empt the scholars; imprisonment is deliberate)

In the ancient myth of Prism, the gods were the first to wield the powers of Life and Death, shaping reality through their control of White and Black magic. For eons, they maintained the world's delicate balance, ensuring that creation and destruction followed the natural order. However, the rise of the rebellious scholars threatened this divine rule. These scholars, imprisoned for their heretical desire to explore beyond the binary magic of the gods, discovered ways to extract and manipulate light and shadow, creating the forces of Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, and Blue.

The gods watched in alarm as the scholars' newfound magics began to unravel the natural order. These new forces—fire, chaos, lightning, nature, and water—threatened to destabilize the delicate equilibrium of Life and Death. Fearing that the scholars' unchecked ambition would bring about the world's destruction, the gods made a dire choice. Rather than allowing the scholars' magic to consume all of creation, the gods shattered the world into seven shards, dividing its magic and power.

Each shard was imbued with one of the magics the scholars had uncovered. The gods imprisoned the rebellious scholars within these realms, sealing them away from one another to prevent their combined powers from threatening the universe again. These shards became the prison realms of Prism, with each realm guarded by a god, now transformed into a warden overseeing not just the magic, but the scholars confined within.

The gateways between the shards are fiercely protected, ensuring that no one can cross between realms without divine permission. The gods, in their roles as prison wardens, ensure that the magic within each shard remains isolated, preventing any single force from growing too powerful and keeping the scholars confined to their respective domains. In this way, the world of Prism endures, divided yet protected, its secrets guarded by the very gods who once sought to preserve creation itself from those who dared to tamper with its foundations.

## Reconciliation notes (editorial — NOT source text, NOT canon; for the adjudication queue)

These are flags for later reconciliation. They are deliberately left open per the recompile
Prime Directive (capture and flag; do not resolve).

- **v3 supersedes v1/v2 on the shattering.** Per Elideus's correction 2, the **gods**
  shattered the world *deliberately, to pre-empt* the scholars — the shattering is an act of
  containment, not an accidental byproduct of the scholars' magic (v1/v2 had it as strain/
  accident that the gods then exploited). The seven shards are a **prison by design**; the
  gods are **wardens**; the **gateways between realms are the guarded thing**.
- **"Scholars" vs. "the Lonely Muse" — [RESOLVED 2026-07-03 by Elideus].** The Muse was
  **one of the rebellious scholars** originally, so the two accounts are the **same
  event-complex**, not rival myths: the scholars discovered the magics beyond White/Black,
  the Muse was among them and became the one who mastered all seven, and that precipitated
  the gods'/Titans' deliberate shattering. Still OPEN (finer): "gods" (this myth) vs.
  "Titans"/"Titan Council" (founding myth) — same authority under two names? Ledger decision
  recorded.
- **Primordial "White magic = the magic of life" — [RESOLVED 2026-07-03 by Elideus].**
  "White = Life" is the **old** framing; canon is **White = Light and Prophecy**, and
  **Life is Green** (god of the vine). The v1 opening's "White magic, the magic of life" is
  therefore **legacy usage** — preserved verbatim here as an old telling, but any book-level
  use maps the life-aspect to Green and reserves White for Light/Prophecy. Still OPEN
  (finer, downstream): because White ≠ Life, the myth's primordial White/Black = Life/Death
  pairing is itself legacy; what the primordial duality re-colors to (e.g. Light/Dark) is
  not yet ruled — do not resolve here. Ledger decision recorded.
- **Color discovery order.** This myth gives a temporal discovery order: Red (fire) → Orange
  (chaos) → Yellow (lightning/air) → Green (nature) → Blue (water), all extracted from the
  White/Black boundary. Book V's genealogy is combinatorial (White → Black/Orange →
  Green/Yellow → Red/Blue). These describe different things (order-of-discovery vs.
  parentage), but any implied ordering should be checked against Book V. Note also the
  gloss pairs here — Red/Pyromancy, Orange/Chaos, Yellow/lightning-and-air, Green/nature,
  Blue/water-and-"the flow of time" — vs. the two-domain school canon (e.g. Red = Fire and
  Blood).
- **Seven shards, five discovered colors.** The scholars discover five colors (R,O,Y,G,B);
  White and Black are the gods' original pair. Seven shards result. Whether White and Black
  each get a shard/warden (making the gods both jailers and shard-holders) or the seven are
  R,O,Y,G,B + White + Black is implied but not stated — worth pinning against the
  seven-planets / seven-schools spine.
