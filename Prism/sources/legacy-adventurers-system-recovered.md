## Book I: Adventurer's System

This book explores the foundational character systems of the game, including lifecycle progression, family legacy, mortality mechanics, and the broader societal roles characters can fulfill. Adventurers are not merely disposable avatars, but permanent threads in the world's evolving Tapestry.

### I. Legacy and Lineage: The Tapestry of Blood

Each player has a "Tapestry View"—a visual and narrative timeline showing every character they've created. Characters age, retire, and eventually die, but their stories endure.

**Deceased Characters:** Become grayscale portraits in the Tapestry. Their final acts are recorded.

**Spectral Echoes:** In rare cases, characters may return as ghosts—either temporarily playable or visible to descendants.

**Inheritance System:** Family lines pass on heirlooms, journals, spells, titles, and unfinished quests. Traits may also be inherited or diluted across generations.

The Tapestry system turns each player's journey into a personal mythos, where every character adds depth to the house's story.

### II. Lifecycle and Mortality

Characters in Prism are born, live, and die. Lifespan varies by race and circumstances.

**Accelerated Time:** The game world runs on a scaled timeline (e.g., 1 second = 1 minute in-world).

**Aging Factors:** Use of wild magic, psionics, curses, or resurrection may accelerate aging or destabilize a character.

**Natural Retirement:** Characters can retire, transitioning from playable to non-playable but still influential within the world.

**Heroic Deaths:** Players may choose to end a character's arc in a dramatic moment to gain legacy bonuses.

#### Death and Respawn Mechanics

Despite the emphasis on mortality, death in combat or through gameplay mishaps does not end a character's journey. Characters can die and respawn under normal circumstances.

**Standard Death:** Upon dying, a character may return to life via a standard respawn mechanic, ghost-walking to their corpse, or other in-world recovery systems.

**No Permadeath in Combat:** Combat death is not final. Players are encouraged to take risks and learn through failure.

**Final Death Through Age:** True, irreversible death occurs through the natural progression of time. Characters eventually grow old and die, with their final moments recorded in the Tapestry.

#### Permanent Characters

Characters cannot be deleted. Once created, a character becomes a fixed part of the world's narrative. This design elevates each decision, making even the act of beginning a new life a permanent and weighty choice. It enforces a mindset of reverence and responsibility, transforming the traditional MMO "alt" into a true character with lasting consequences.

**No Restarts:** Players cannot erase failure by deleting a character. Mistakes, victories, and losses all persist.

**Hardcore Mode Philosophy:** Similar in spirit to hardcore RPGs, the permanence of death and legacy gives meaning to risk and achievement.

**Tapestry Multiplicity:** Players may have multiple Tapestries, allowing for different family lines, playstyles, or themes—but these, too, are limited. Each Tapestry is a separate legacy.

**Bound by the World:** Every character ever created leaves an echo in the game world—whether as a grave, a ghost, or a name spoken in legend.

#### Families and Offspring

The family system enables the organic expansion of a player's legacy through offspring. Reproduction is handled through a symbolic interaction with the Tapestry rather than any simulated biology.

**Offspring Selection:** Players may choose to "add an offspring" to their lineage by interacting with their Tapestry interface in-game. Gender or romantic pairing is not required; inclusivity and narrative freedom are prioritized.

**Incubation Timer:** Upon selection, a timer begins for the child's arrival, representing natural passage of time.

**Inheritance and Influence:** Newborn characters may inherit traits, gear, or quests. Their growth may be accelerated to allow them to become playable within a reasonable in-game timespan.

As characters age, they often move away from adventuring and into more specialized or passive roles:

- **Youth:** Adventurers, explorers, mercenaries.
- **Middle Age:** Crafters, strategists, architects, governors.
- **Old Age:** Scholars, sages, merchants, or eccentric hermits.
- **Elder Statesmen:** Manor lords, cult founders, or retired legends with rare influence.

Older characters may also transition into automated roles. A powerful blacksmith might retire from adventuring and become a shopkeeper, vendor, or overseer of production. These automated characters continue to influence the world—crafting, trading, or researching on behalf of the player's legacy.

The overworld building system allows players to construct settlements, infrastructure, and production chains comparable to builder games like Satisfactory. Retired characters can be assigned to run these operations, adding personal narrative depth and mechanical integration to economic gameplay.

Each stage brings new gameplay opportunities and shifting roles, encouraging long-term attachment to characters and their evolving place in the world.

Mortality is a central mechanic—death matters. But it also unlocks new systems. The permanence of characters ensures that nothing is trivial.

### III. Roles Beyond Combat

Characters need not all be adventurers. The system supports:

- **Crafters:** Smiths, alchemists, scribes.
- **Architects:** Designers of dungeons, buildings, and magical constructs.
- **Traders:** Players who influence markets and supply chains.
- **Scholars:** Researchers who contribute to the discovery of new magic or technologies.

These non-combat roles generate value for family legacies and guilds. Retired characters may continue to operate in these roles, offering services or mentorship. Characters can also be assigned to automated tasks within the overworld builder system, continuing their life's work as self-sustaining NPCs.

### IV. Ascension and Immortality

Some characters transcend death. In the Tapestry UI, characters who reach the end of their natural lifespan transition to grayscale portraits, marking them as "ghost state" by default. This state does not mean final disconnection—rather, it represents their shift from living presence to legacy entity.

Ghost-state characters remain interactable through the use of widely available mid-tier magic. Spells to summon or commune with deceased characters are commonplace, making it easy for players to speak with their elders, recover guidance, or retrieve hidden knowledge tied to a specific legacy.

Ghosts can also be affected by ongoing player actions:

- **Honoring the Dead:** Erecting statues, dedicating places, or performing rituals can empower ghost-state characters.
- **Tethering:** Ghosts may be bound to specific objects, buildings, or locations.
- **Possession or Projection:** High-tier rituals may allow temporary embodiment or influence over current events.

#### Divine Ascension and Immortalization

- **Divine Patronage:** Ascension through legendary deeds, earning godhood or demigod status.
- **Personal Ascension:** A one-time, server-wide ascension path available to a single player per world. Triggered by a unique feat or event, this transforms the character into a permanent global deity, enshrined in the lore and mechanics of the game. *(Intake 2026-07-13: DROPPED — superseded by the Muse's path.)*
- **Mythic Resurrection:** Powerful quests or rituals may return a character to life with permanent changes.
- **Eternal Guardians:** Characters bound to a place or object, serving as protectors or fonts of wisdom.
- **Permanent Haunting:** A character's spirit becomes an unremovable, cursed presence in the world—interacting with players, affecting events, and shaping locations indefinitely.
- **Imbuement:** A character's soul may be fused with a magical object or relic, granting it sentience and unique powers. These items may speak, influence events, or require reverence from future wielders.

These paths are rare, difficult, and meaningful. Immortal characters are rendered in the Tapestry in radiant or color-shifted overlays, distinguishing them from standard ghost portraits. They may continue to operate in the world as NPCs, deities, or lore-bearing mythological figures. Interacting with these characters may require specialized access or divine magic.

#### Tapestry Management and Merging

Player ownership of items and assets is tied to the account—not any single Tapestry or character. However, asset use and control still respect Tapestry boundaries. Players can:

- **Split a Tapestry:** Branch one family line into a new slot, freeing legacy space.
- **Merge Tapestries:** Combine two or more family lines into a single narrative lineage.

This allows players to manage up to five distinct Tapestries with flexibility—exploring different cultural themes, magical disciplines, or political roles—without ever deleting or discarding past stories. Immortal characters may become persistent NPCs in the world or appear in other players' stories.

### V. Narrative Hooks Through Bloodlines

Family and legacy aren't cosmetic—they influence gameplay.

- **Locked Areas:** Accessible only by characters of a particular bloodline.
- **Ancestral Quests:** Story arcs that unfold across generations.
- **Resurfacing Relics:** Artifacts tied to past characters may be recovered and reactivated.
- **Ghost Guidance:** Deceased characters may appear to descendants in visions, dreamscapes, or as passive buffs.

Legacy characters become part of the world's history, recorded and visible to others. In doing so, they provide unique narrative leverage and emergent storylines.
